# DoctorAI World promotion research

Updated 6 October 2026 after checking current primary sources and the latest protected Preview. This is a practical, low-risk promotion plan for the current website candidate. It assumes no launch market, advertising budget, clinical review, or legal approval has been confirmed. New Zealand is used only as a working example because the site and draft plan contain NZ-specific content.

## Recommended starting position

Lead with the free appointment-preparation checklist as a small, specific task: **make a short list of what to bring and what to ask at the next healthcare visit**. Offer the Health Hub as an optional next step. Keep the message about organisation and preparation; do not lead with “AI doctor,” diagnosis, triage, medicine safety, clinical endorsement, or health outcomes.

Health New Zealand already offers public guidance to prepare for a healthcare appointment, including writing down concerns, bringing a medicine list and relevant documents, asking questions, and noting next steps. DoctorAI should complement that guidance with an editable, printable tool and clearly link to the public source; it should not imply that the product replaces a clinician or official service. Verify each checklist prompt against current local guidance before publication. [Health New Zealand: preparing for your healthcare provider visit](https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/hospitals-services/services-support/general-practices/preparing-for-your-doctors-visit)

Use one landing page and one call to action. A simple route is:

1. Visitor opens the appointment checklist.
2. Visitor chooses what to add and reviews the result.
3. Visitor prints or saves it.
4. Visitor may open the Health Hub if they want to organise more notes.

Explain storage and sharing at the point where the user makes that choice. Avoid “private,” “secure,” or “confidential” as broad marketing promises until the real account, storage, processor and deletion paths have been reviewed.

## What the research changes

### Search should build trust and answer one job

Google recommends helpful, original, people-first content. Its Search guidance says its systems give more weight to strong experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust signals for topics that can affect health or safety. That does not make a named clinician a ranking requirement, but it supports a careful publication process: identify the author and reviewer when accurate, show a review date, cite primary local sources, describe limits plainly, and correct outdated material. Do not publish a high volume of near-duplicate AI-generated health pages. [Google Search Central: helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

The initial content cluster should stay narrow:

- appointment preparation checklist for the confirmed market;
- questions to take to a healthcare visit;
- what information or documents a person may want to bring;
- how to note the agreed next step after a visit.

Keep these pages practical, original, short enough to use, accessible on a phone, and linked to dated primary sources. Do not turn the checklist into symptom or medicine-safety advice. Add a source link to Health New Zealand where NZ content is in scope; do not copy its page wholesale.

Once the public release is stable, verify the domain in Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, inspect the main landing page, and check indexing. Review Search Console clicks, impressions, click-through rate, query, landing page, country and device. Use these patterns to improve page titles, snippets and the actual task flow; do not chase absolute average position or create pages for every keyword. Search Console reports search discovery before a visitor lands, while Analytics reports on-site activity; the measures will not match exactly. [Google Search Central: using Search Console and Analytics data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console)

### Health advertising rules reach owned channels too

If New Zealand is confirmed, the updated ASA Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code took effect for new ads on 1 April 2026 and for all ads on 1 July 2026. It covers websites, social posts, influencers, apps, email and other media, including advertiser-controlled channels. Claims must be truthful and substantiated before publication; testimonials and endorsements have specific limits. Have a qualified NZ adviser classify the product and review the complete ad, landing page and claims together. [ASA: Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code](https://asa.co.nz/codes/codes/therapeutic-and-health-advertising-code-new/), [ASA: health advertising resources](https://asa.co.nz/industry-guidance/therapeutic-and-health-advertising-resources/)

Treat every public asset as a claim: homepage copy, metadata, Play listing, social posts, influencer material, testimonials, screenshots and ads. Do not say or imply that DoctorAI improves health, saves clinician time, catches a condition, selects safe medicines, or is endorsed unless qualified review and suitable evidence support that exact claim and local rules permit it. Do not rely on a disclaimer to correct a misleading headline.

### Paid search can start with intent, not health profiles

Google classifies personal-health content as a sensitive interest category for personalised advertising. Its policy restricts advertiser-curated audiences for sensitive categories. For an initial campaign, use contextual Search intent only, with no remarketing, Customer Match, lookalike or other health-derived audience lists. Keep the targeting tied to the search task, use the confirmed adult market, and check current platform eligibility before creating a campaign. [Google Ads: health in personalised advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855), [Google Ads: restricted targeting in personalised advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)

If the release gates turn green, test tightly relevant searches such as “doctor appointment checklist” and the equivalent wording used in the chosen country. Exclude symptom-diagnosis, emergency, treatment, medicine-interaction and “AI doctor” intent. Do not target a person because they visited a health page. Set a spend cap and stop rule chosen by the owner before launch; there is no evidence here to justify a particular budget.

Before placing any analytics or advertising tags, have privacy counsel review exactly what each tag receives. Do not send typed questions, symptoms, medicine names, document contents, account email, or a health-derived audience signal to an advertising platform. Use aggregate, non-sensitive events such as a generic checklist-start and checklist-complete count only after consent, data-flow and retention review. The NZ Office of the Privacy Commissioner publishes the Health Information Privacy Code in force from 1 May 2026; obtain advice on whether and how its rules apply to DoctorAI's actual role and data flows. [Office of the Privacy Commissioner: Health Information Privacy Code 2020](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/codes-of-practice/hipc2020/)

## Promotion sequence

### Days 0–7: make the destination trustworthy

- Confirm the country, operator, adult scope, support contact and intended purpose.
- Publish the reviewed checklist-led landing page only after its public copy and privacy flow are approved.
- Make the public Privacy Notice, Terms, support contact and account-deletion URL accurate and reachable. Check the public URL directly; a local or protected Preview page is not proof that the public site is ready.
- Have qualified clinical, privacy and legal reviewers approve the medication and AI claims for the confirmed market.
- Keep paid advertising off while the public site, deletion route, storage, support and product claims remain unresolved.

### Days 8–28 (weeks 2–4): validate the task without collecting health records

- Run 5–10 short sessions with adults in the chosen market. Ask participants to use fictional details.
- Watch whether they understand what the checklist does, can finish it on a phone, can print or save it, and know what happens to their information.
- Record usability observations, not diagnoses or medicine lists. Fix the largest comprehension and accessibility issues first.
- Publish one carefully sourced guide and share it from an owner-controlled profile. Ask permission before distributing through an organisation or using its logo.

### Days 29–56 (weeks 5–8): improve organic discovery

- Review Search Console query and landing-page patterns monthly; compare country and device when market scope is known.
- Improve the checklist page based on real query wording and observed task friction.
- Publish one source-checked resource a month and reuse it in one or two short, accessible posts a week.
- Do not scale content output until a qualified reviewer can maintain its quality.

### Days 57–84 (weeks 9–12): decide whether to test paid Search

- Re-check every website, clinical, privacy, legal, support, billing and measurement gate.
- If each gate is approved, run a small, owner-capped contextual Search pilot to the checklist page. Use no health-derived audiences or retargeting.
- Judge the pilot by relevant searches, completed checklist tasks, user comprehension and support burden. Do not treat clicks or sign-ups alone as proof of health benefit or product-market fit.
- Stop if claims are misunderstood, support cannot keep up, tracking captures health information, or the cost cap is reached without useful task completion.

### Days 85–90: close the loop

- Reconcile actual spend, relevant search terms, checklist completion counts, user comprehension feedback, accessibility issues and support load against the owner's pre-set thresholds.
- Record which claims, audiences, destinations and data flows were actually used. Remove expired ads and access that is no longer needed.
- Decide in writing to stop, revise the offer, or continue within a newly approved cap. Keep the campaign paused if a release gate regressed or no owner is available to monitor it.

## Ready-to-review launch assets

These are drafts for the confirmed market and require the same clinical/legal/privacy review as the site. Keep one offer and one destination, `/appointment-checklist`.

**Search ad draft**

- Headline: Free Doctor Visit Checklist
- Headline: Prepare for Your Appointment
- Description: Organise the topics and questions you want to raise at your next appointment. Use or print the free checklist.
- Destination: the reviewed appointment checklist page.
- Keyword themes to consider after market confirmation: “doctor appointment checklist”, “questions to ask at a doctor visit”, and local equivalents. Exclude emergency, diagnosis, symptom checker, treatment, and medicine-interaction intent. Do not add remarketing or uploaded customer lists.

**Organic post draft**

> Getting ready for a healthcare appointment? It can help to write down what you want to discuss and the questions you want to ask. DoctorAI World's free checklist gives you a place to organise those notes before your visit: [link]. It is an organising tool, not medical advice.

**Measurement plan**

- Before paid traffic, use Search Console to check the checklist page's index status, search queries, country and device. Treat those reports as discovery data, not health outcomes.
- If a qualified privacy reviewer approves analytics, count only generic events such as `checklist_start` and `checklist_print`; do not attach text, symptoms, medicines, account identifiers or health-derived audiences. Record consent, retention, access and the campaign stop rule before installing a tag.
- Set the daily/total spend cap, target cost per completed checklist, support capacity and stop threshold with the owner. Leave all numeric caps blank until approved; clicks and registrations alone are not success criteria.
- Keep a dated copy of the final ad, destination, targeting, consent/data-flow decision, approvals and results. Pause if the page changes, the audience rules change, a claim is misunderstood, tracking receives health information or support cannot keep up.
