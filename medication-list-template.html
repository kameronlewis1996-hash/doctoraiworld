    What to put on the list

    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration recommends keeping a current list of medicines and sharing it with health professionals. Its patient guidance includes prescription and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and supplements.

    • Include the medicines and supplements you use, including ones you take only sometimes.
    • Copy the medicine name, strength, and directions from the label when available. Add what you take it for in your own words and write down questions if a detail is unclear.
    • Update your notes when your medicines change. Do not guess at missing details; ask your pharmacist or care team to help check them.
    • Take the list to your appointment or pharmacy and review it with a health professional.

    This template is for organizing information and questions. It does not tell you what medicine to take or how to take it. Do not start, stop, or change a medicine based on this page.

    Questions about this template

    Does DoctorAI check whether medicines are safe together?

    This blank template does not check medicine safety. DoctorAI Pro has a separate, limited database check that can flag some known medicine clashes, duplicate ingredients, and recorded allergy matches. An absent alert does not mean medicines are safe together. Ask a pharmacist or clinician to review your full list before making treatment decisions.

    Should I include vitamins or supplements?

    FDA patient guidance recommends listing vitamins and supplements as well as prescription and over-the-counter medicines. Ask your care team what details they need.

    Where can I learn more?

    Read the FDA guide to creating and keeping a medication list and the MedlinePlus guide to keeping medicines organized. This page summarizes general organizational guidance; it is not a substitute for personal medical advice.
